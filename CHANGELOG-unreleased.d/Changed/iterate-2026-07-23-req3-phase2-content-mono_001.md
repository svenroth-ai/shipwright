**Every requirement now states what it actually guarantees.** The requirements catalog went from ~90 acceptance criteria to 196: each of the eighteen requirements was walked against the code that implements it - not against the documents that describe it - and its criteria rewritten to say what must be true when the capability has done its job, what it refuses, and what it deliberately will not do. Nine statements that read as guarantees turned out to be untrue and were corrected, among them two safety nets reporting success while doing nothing. `/shipwright-grade` was minted as a requirement of its own: it had shipped, been published, and had none. Every criterion now carries an enforcement classification - enforced, enforced-but-untested, instruction-only-and-checkable, instruction-only-and-not-checkable, or not built at all - so the follow-up work can be picked up per criterion instead of per guess.
