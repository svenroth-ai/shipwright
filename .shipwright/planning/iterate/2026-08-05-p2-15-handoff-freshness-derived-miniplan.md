## Alternative approach considered — and why rejected

**Re-stamp the marker after the integrate**, inside `integrate_regenerate` /
`ensure_current`, which already knows `--run-id`: after the restore, write a fresh canon
marker for this run. Smaller conceptual footprint, touches no registry, and repairs the
damage at the site that caused it.

**Rejected on safety.** A re-stamp *manufactures* canon evidence: the marker's meaning is
"this phase's C3 closure happened", and `ensure_current` cannot know whether F5b ran — it
takes a `--run-id` and nothing else. Invoked before F5b (or by any future caller), it
would forge a C3 pass for a phase that wrote nothing, which is precisely the staleness C3
exists to detect (`handoff_phase_canon.py:23`). The chosen approach only ever *preserves
bytes that already existed*: if F5b never ran, there is nothing to carry and both checks
correctly stay red. Preserving evidence beats reconstructing it.

Secondary reason: a re-stamp leaves the file tracked-and-dirty across the merge without
the stash, reintroducing the "local changes would be overwritten by merge" abort that
`run_written_ledger.py:15` documents as measured, not theorised.

## Risks and how they are covered

| Risk | Cover |
|---|---|
| The handoff now stays dirty for the rest of the run, and a later merge refuses. | That is exactly what `stash_run_written` prevents, and step 4 exercises the real merge rather than asserting it. |
| Someone later "harmonises" `compliance_refresh.CLASSIFICATION` to match. | One sentence at the carve-out naming the two axes and why they differ. |
| The carve-out reads as "the handoff may ship now". | AC4's negative test, plus the docstring keeping the path in `DERIVED_SNAPSHOTS`. |
| `integrate_regenerate`'s failure rollback no longer covers the handoff. | Correct by construction — it is not regenerated there (`only=set()`), and the run's bytes are written back in `integrate_main`'s `finally`. Asserted in step 4. |
